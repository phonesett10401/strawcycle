Where the smoke comes from

Why straw gets burned after harvest, and what buying it changes.

The smoke season

After harvest, many northern Thai farmers burn the straw left in the field. It is the cheapest and fastest way to clear it before the next crop.

  • Crop-residue burning in the north runs from about December to April, and PM2.5 is highest in January, February and March, says IQAir, “Seasonal crop burning pollutes Thailand’s skies”.
  • The same article reports that in 2020 the January to March average ran roughly three to nine times the yearly average in the cities it tracked.
  • Burning straw also releases greenhouse gases and harms health, according to GIZ Thailand’s announcement of its Urmatt partnership.

Straw is not the only smoke: forest and other crop fires add to it. We do not claim that buying straw ends the haze.

How the money reaches the farmer

Three steps. The farmer is paid for the straw, so keeping it has a value.

  1. 1

    A business pays

    A restaurant buys cartons of packaging, or a person sponsors a field at ฿99 per rai.

  2. 2

    The farmer is paid per kilo

    The farmer bales the straw and is paid for each kilo instead of burning it.

  3. 3

    Straw is pressed into packaging

    The straw is pressed into boxes, bowls and cups that go back to the restaurant.

This is the model we are proposing. The price per kilo is not set yet, so we show no figure for it.

Our numbers, and where they come from

These are the figures used across this site. None comes from our own measurements.

650 kg of straw per rai
Our planning estimate; not measured by us. Published figures vary with rice variety, season and how the straw is cut.
1 rai = 1,600 m²
The standard Thai unit of land area.
20 g of straw per 750 ml meal box
An estimate for our planned box. Other product weights are also estimates until samples are weighed.
฿99 per rai
The sponsorship price in this class project. It is a demo, so no money moves.

Worked example, using the estimates above: 650 kg of straw would fill about 32,500 meal boxes of 20 g each, before any loss in processing.
